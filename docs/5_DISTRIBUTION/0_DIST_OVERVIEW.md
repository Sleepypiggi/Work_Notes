# **Distribution Overview**

An age old of addage of the insurance industry is that “Insurance is sold, not bought”. Few people realize the need for insurance on their own accord - thus insurers employ dedicated salesforce to sell insurance.

!!! Warning

    It is often the case that the people who realize that they need insurance (likely due to falling ill) are those who can no longer get it, because they have become uninsurable.
    
Most insurers today use multiple sales channels, each best at targeting a specific target market.

For simplicity, this section is through the lens of the **Agency** channel, which has historically been the largest distribution channel in the industry. Subsequent sections will cover the other distribution channels and differences compared to Agency.

## **Sales Process**

The typical sales process has 4 key phases:

1. Prospecting
2. Needs Analysis
3. Presenting & Closing
4. Managing

Prospecting refers to the process of **finding clients** to purchase insurance:

* Initially, this is an **explicit process** via cold calling, roadshows or other methods
* Eventually, existing clients will provide **recommendations** to their social circle; virtually eliminating the need explicitly prospect
* Some insurers might also help **generate leads**

Needs Analysis refers to talking to the prospect to **understand their lifestyle & financial needs/goals** and thus determine which product(s) they would be potentially interested in.

* Most insurers have developed a variety of tools and resources to aide in this process

Presenting refers to the process of recommending a specific product to the prospect:

* Given the complexity of modern life insurance, a **sales illustration** is typically provided to allow the customer to better understand how the product will function
* Sales illustrations are highly regulated, often **limiting specific parameters** of the projection (EG. Illustrated growth rate) and requiring **specific language or disclosures**
 
Closing refers to the process of getting the prospect to submit an application, undergo medical underwriting and be issued the policy:

* The product will be issued in the form of a **legal contract** known as an **insurance policy**
* The salesperson will need to go explain the terms, features and options of the policy
* Given the complexity of the purchase, most jurisdictions provide a short **Freelook period** following policy issuance where the client can **rescind their purchase** without consequence
* The salesperson has a key role to **reinforce** their new clients decision to purchase

!!! Note

    Most new clients experience some degree of **Buyer’s Remorse**, due to the large financial commitment involved in purchasing insurance.
    
Servicing refers to assisting the client with a wide range of activities post-issuance:

* Submitting claims, updating details, requesting for policy values, policy loans etc
* Most insurers today have a **dedicated function** to assist the clients with such matters, allowing the salesperson to **focus more on prospecting**
* However, most salespeople want to be **kept in the loop** of such activities as they indicate changing needs for their client, which could be a **potential sales opportunity** as well
